Save the Date

Nov 10, 2026

Villagers now have projects, routines and invitations, and they ask you to be part of them.

New

Save the Date: every villager's project

At 8 hearts, a villager can ask you to help with a project of their own. The project becomes an arc with missions, and the arc ends in a festival on the game's calendar.

The proposal

  • From 8, every regular conversation with the villager has a chance (30% by default) of turning into their proposal, in a dialogue box with the project's colour, banner and music.
  • Every Save the Date conversation is easy to spot: the reply box takes the project's colour, with a Special conversation ribbon, the project's name and a heart in each corner.
  • Say yes and a heart arc opens. Each villager proposes once per save. After a “no” (or an abandoned arc), they bring it up one more time in a later season, and you can pick it back up from the action wheel whenever you like. Up to three arcs can be open at once.

The missions

  • On the Arcs screen, the project gets its own section at the top, in its colour, with the required missions, the bonus ones and who's involved.
  • You talk each mission through with the right villager from the action wheel, once per villager per day, and the AI decides whether they're in. A regular conversation never completes a mission. With the wheel turned off, the same choice shows up as a bonus action in the reply box.
  • A bonus mission never blocks the event, but it changes something on the day: a food stall, a referee, a jukebox, a pizza on the table.

On the calendar

  • Once the required missions are done, Schedule offers up to three of the nearest free days, 2 to 15 days ahead. Never on a festival (the game's, another mod's or another Save the Date event), never on the green rain day, and a birthday only when no other day is left.
  • The invitation arrives in the mail the next morning, the Weekly News announces it, the calendar marks the day with a ticket in the project's colour, and on the eve the host reminds you on the Messenger (or with a note, if they don't have a phone).
  • Daytime events run from 10 AM to 4 PM, evening ones from 7 PM to midnight, and Sam's follows the JojaMart's hours.
  • Up to the eve, Postpone on the host's wheel moves the event once to the next free day, with a new invitation.

The day

  • The festival happens on the real map, with the cast in place and an AI line for everyone you talk to. Talk to the host twice to start, then pick the difficulty and the controls.
  • Losing still counts as held. Leaving in the middle asks first and counts as a loss. There's no second try the same day.
  • After a short closing ceremony the day goes on: the clock moves to two hours after you arrived, you're at the map's entrance, the villagers go back to their routines and the shops are open. Your spouse is awake at home, and the farm's machines only run for the time you spent at the festival.
  • Walking off the map before it starts (the game's Leave the festival?) just sends you home: the clock doesn't move and the event stays open until it closes.
  • Missed it? The host moves the event to the next free day. Going to bed before an evening event also just moves it.

What stays

  • For a week, the first conversation with the host and with up to four of the cast is about the event: the result, who stood out and what it meant to them.
  • The villagers remember, the night's diary and gossip talk about the event, and so do the Weekly News and the Year in Review.
  • The Hub's 💌 Save the Date tab (F9) is a festival notice board: a cork board under a string of bunting, with a poster for each villager. The poster has the photo, the title and a sticker that says where the project stands (2/3 missions, Today!, Tomorrow, the date, Held). Anyone you haven't reached yet is a blank sheet with a silhouette.
  • The poster of a held event opens its postcard: the host's portrait in a polaroid (smiling after a win), the score of the last edition, who stood out and a medal for each edition: gold for a win, silver for a draw, bronze for a loss.

Yearly festival

  • From the postcard, the event can become a yearly festival on the same date. Only the festival with the minigame comes back, without the arc.
  • Every edition is kept (“3 editions: 2 won, 1 lost”), the villagers and the ceremony remember the earlier ones, and after a win the rivals want a rematch: the next edition suggests one difficulty higher.
  • If something the event needs stops existing (the JojaMart closing for good), it happens one last time, and the postcard says goodbye instead of offering the yearly festival.

Eight festivals

Every project ends in a festival with its own minigame, played on the map where it would really happen.

The Clearing Cup Alex

Four-a-side football on the Flower Dance clearing, the Pelicanos (your team) against Alex's Saloon, in front of 28 villagers. Kent keeps goal from year 2; before that, Clint does. A bonus mission opens a practice drill with Alex, and finishing the drill makes you a little faster on the day. I ported the match from Beach Episode.

The Jungle Splat Abigail

Four-a-side paintball in the jungle of Ginger Island, in one-hit elimination rounds, best of five. Willy's part of the arc waits for his boat and the jungle path, and the arc, the wheel and the album say what's missing. Also ported from Beach Episode. Finishing Robin's training circuit gives you a spare paint balloon every round.

The Field Day Vincent and Jas

Best-of-three dodgeball on the town square, with Penny as referee. Either kid can propose it, and every mission goes through a grown-up. The kids pick teams with odds or evens, and the grown-ups go easy on them: no super throws at a kid, who is just “out”.

Dance Night at the Saloon Emily

A dance-off on the Saloon floor at night: Emily dances a sequence and you repeat it, over three rounds, with the crowd bar deciding each one. Clint can be the judge and Pam can run the jukebox.

The Saloon Anniversary Gus

A night waiting tables in the real Saloon: Gus cooks and you carry each dish to the right table before the guest's patience runs out, over three busier and busier rounds. Pam, Shane, Lewis, Linus and Clint each have their own quirk, and a guest who gives up waiting says so in their own way.

The JojaMart Cart Championship Sam

Sam's last shift at the JojaMart, played as Snake on the store floor: you and Sam push ever longer lines of carts through the aisles while Morris makes his rounds. Shane gets Sam the shift, and Vincent (cart boy) and Pam (keeping Morris busy) can help.

The Chicken Race Shane

Three laps around Marnie's ranch, on the regular Forest map. Shane races Charlie, his blue chicken. You enter an animal from your own coop, rated by friendship, happiness and age, or Marnie lends you a chicken. Ostriches, dinosaurs, rabbits and ducks can race too, and the crowd has something to say about each one.

The RPG Campaign Sebastian

A night of Solarion Chronicles in his room, with Sam and Abigail: three rooms and a boss in a short run of Stardew Vanguard, my survivors-style mine game, with Sebastian narrating as game master between the rooms and his friends reacting. Leaving the farm in the evening takes you straight to his room.

And three standalone events

Save the Date events that don't come from an arc: the villager invites you, the day goes on the calendar with a letter and a reminder, and at the end there's a group photo, the album and Pelicangram.

The band's open rehearsal Sam · 7 hearts

Once a season, on a Thursday, Sam tells you the garage is open on Saturday and his mom said it's fine. Ten minutes later, Jodi tells you it's fine until 4 PM. The band plays in Sam's room for Jodi, Vincent, Jas, Penny, Haley and, from year 2, Kent, and it all ends in a photo. No minigame for now.

The chess open Demetrius · 4 hearts

At 4 hearts, once a season, on a Wednesday, Demetrius texts you that there's an open on the town square on Saturday, from 10 AM to 4 PM, and that he already entered you. On Saturday the square gets four wooden tables with boards and the open's banner. Penny, Maru, Harvey, George, Sebastian, Elliott and Demetrius are at the tables, and Robin, Evelyn and Lewis watch.

Talk to Demetrius to start: you sit at the first table and the board opens on screen. It's an eight-player knockout: in the quarterfinals you face Penny, then come the semifinal and the final. You play white in the quarterfinal and the final and black in the semi, and a draw sends black through. The other tables play out while you play and fill in the bracket.

Pick the piece and then the square, with the mouse, touch or a controller. You can offer a draw, resign or leave from the pause menu (leaving counts as a loss). There are three difficulties, the villagers play stronger every round, and Maru, Sebastian and Demetrius take it a bit more seriously. All the rules apply, castling, en passant and promotion included.

At the end there's a group photo, the album and Pelicangram, friendship (+20 with Demetrius, +30 if you're champion and +10 with each villager you played) and a diary entry. The Weekly News and the Year in Review keep your first open and your first title. If you miss it, Demetrius texts you that Penny went through by walkover, and invites you again next season.

A Day on Ginger Island Emily · 6 hearts

With Willy's boat fixed, Emily suggests a day on the island with her sister and Willy, who steers because someone has to. Emily waits at the parrots' perch, Haley sunbathes on a towel and Willy fishes from the dock. Once you've talked to all three, everyone gathers for a photo, which becomes Haley's post on Pelicangram. No minigame and no score.

31 side jobs: work that comes from friendship

As your friendship grows, a villager may offer you a job. Each side job has pay, rules of its own and stages that open up with more hearts.

  • The offer comes in a regular conversation, in a box in the job's colour with a free answer, like a Save the Date proposal (with the same Chance to propose). Said no? The villager asks again next season, or you bring it up from their action wheel.
  • Many side jobs come with an app for the phone and for the computer's Apps folder, with the history and the numbers.
  • Everything leaves a mark: the villagers remember, the night's diary and gossip comment on it, the Weekly News announces the first times and the big milestones, and the Year in Review keeps the day you started.
  • The animals, aquariums and shelves are drawn by the mod, without touching the game's maps or save. In multiplayer, only the host has side jobs.

New

Slots and the side jobs board

If you work for half the valley, your phone no longer rings all day: of the side jobs you took, only five run at once, and the rest wait on pause.

  • Pausing loses nothing: a paused side job keeps everything you earned in it (ratings, trust, apps, trophies) and stays quiet, with no messages, no orders and no app, until you turn it back on.
  • What takes a slot: the 22 side jobs that come to you with something to do (PierreGo, Gus's kitchen, Marnie's runaways, Shane's round, the weekly ones). The 6 you start yourself are free: the walks with George and with Dusty, the trades with Linus and with Krobus, the mayor's suggestion box and Kent's bed. Cooking together, the island break with your spouse and Caroline's aerobics are commitments and stay outside the limit.
  • Changes apply tomorrow morning: a side job you pause runs to the end of the day, and one you turn on starts the next morning, if there's a free slot. Pausing one and turning on another on the same day swaps them overnight, and you can undo it until you go to sleep.
  • No punishment: what the side job had asked for and you hadn't done is dropped without counting as missed (Emily's open commissions, the mayor's cases, Jas's theme, Pam's find of the week, Harvey's round, Sebastian's calls). What you already earned gets paid.
  • Every slot taken: the villager still offers and you can still say yes. The new side job starts on pause and a notice says so. Pause another one to make room.
  • Older saves: every side job you already had keeps running, even over the limit. Nothing goes on pause unless you ask, but no new side job starts until the running ones fit the limit.

The side jobs board

  • With any side job turned on, the Save the Date card in the Hub opens a board with two tabs: Festivals (the album from before) and Side jobs. Tab or the controller's triggers switch tabs.
  • Each side job has a poster. Until someone offers it to you, it's a blank sheet with a silhouette and a hint (“A dog behind a fence is dying for a walk.”). The ones you turned down say They asked you, and the ones you took say Running, On pause, Pauses tomorrow or Back tomorrow, with the job's rhythm (every day, a few days a week, once a week, whenever you want). The ones that don't count toward the limit get a Free or Commitment stamp.
  • Pick the poster of one of your side jobs to pause it or turn it on from tomorrow morning, and pick it again to undo. The pennants at the top are the slots: coloured when a side job is running, red when over the limit, and the line below counts them (“Slots: 3 of 5 today, 4 tomorrow”).
  • New on the board: when a villager opens a Save the Date project or offers you a side job, the Save the Date card in the Hub shines with a “!”, like the Changelog card, until you open the board. It opens on the side with the news, the other tab gets a little dot and the poster comes with a New sticker.
  • Side jobs today: the Hub's Today note gets a line with the side jobs that have a task for you (“Side jobs today: PierreGo, Gus's kitchen.”), the Save the Date card counts them, and on the board the poster says Task open. A paused side job never shows up there.

Improved

A new look for the action wheel

The wheel that opens when you click a villager got bigger and got a design of its own.

  • Badges on a wooden hoop: every action is now a badge with an icon, on a wooden hoop with brass rivets: a speech bubble to talk, a gift box, a coin for the shop, a heart for kisses and hugs, a parcel for deliveries and favours, a calendar, a letter, or the face of the villager the action brings along. Give gift shows the item in your hand, and a delivery shows what you're about to hand over.
  • Whole names: every name sits on a paper label, never cut off and never over the portrait.
  • The villager in the middle: their portrait in a carved frame, their name on a red ribbon and their hearts underneath. They smile when you point at a kiss or at a Save the Date project.
  • Pointing at an action: its slice of the wheel lights up and its badge glows. The panel below says what it does, or why you can't do it right now, and what you can't do is greyed out. With more than six actions, arrows show the next page and dots above the portrait tell you which one you're on.

When you get close to a whole family, they invite you to dinner. You answer in your own words in a conversation with everyone at the table at once, and you go home with a plate.

  • Group conversations now seat up to four villagers.

Improved

A calmer Seen and Heard

Seen and Heard was made to season the villagers' lines, not to run them. Some players were being accused of things they never did on purpose, so I reviewed every situation that fires in ordinary play.

  • “You keep hanging around my house”: walking in and out of a shop where someone lives in the back (Pierre's General Store, the Saloon, the Blacksmith, the ranch, the Carpenter's Shop, the Fish Shop) counted as going into their home, and every door was a new memory. Now shops and public rooms are just places, the same door again within 6 in-game hours counts as one visit, and in a conversation the villager only hears about the last one.
  • Flirting and arguing over small talk: the word lists matched pieces of other words and everyday words (“ugly weather”). Now only whole words count, insults have to be aimed at the person, and only the farmer's own words count. The first flirt in front of your partner is a dry speech bubble; only the second brings them over to you.
  • Going through other people's houses: clicking a bookshelf or a poster counted as reading the owner's letters and made them furious. Now only dressers, letters and notes count, and the first time is a speech bubble.
  • Farm work: the hoe no longer counts as hitting someone, and a scythe passing close is a scare, not a blow. An axe or pickaxe upsets instead of infuriating (a weapon still infuriates), and a weapon swung nearby gets a speech bubble before anyone walks over. A bomb on your own farm bothers your spouse much less.
  • Following and staring: following only counts while you walk the same way as the villager, and never on the farm or in your house. Staring waits 4 seconds instead of 3, and the 10 seconds after a conversation, a menu or a cutscene don't count. Finishing a chat and standing still is no longer “Why are you staring at me?”.
  • Bedrooms: going back into the same bedroom within 6 in-game hours carries on the same visit, instead of starting a new one.

Fixes

Fixes

  • A busy minute no longer costs the whole day: Gemini's free tier answers with the same error (429) for its per-minute limit and for its daily quota, and I treated every 429 as the daily quota running out. A few quick conversations, or a morning full of village tasks, could switch the AI off until you slept, and a second key that was invalid did the same. Now the per-minute limit only rests that key for the seconds Gemini asks for: the request moves to your next key, or waits up to 30 seconds and tries again, or fails on its own with a notice to try again shortly. Only the daily quota (or a 429 that doesn't say which limit it is) still falls back to the base game until the next day.
  • Group conversations keep every line: when the AI quoted a word inside a villager's line, the reply was cut down to the last villager's line and the others were lost. Now the quotes are repaired first, and every line of the group shows up.
  • No more scolding for a few days away: since 1.7.4, a villager you hadn't talked to for two days or more was told so in every conversation and wouldn't drop it, sometimes twice in the same chat and in a reproachful tone. Now only the day's opening line hears about it, only after 25 days without talking, and the villager mentions it once, warmly, glad to see you.
  • A table of four lets you reply: at a family dinner or another sit-down talk with four villagers, the AI's opening often took longer than the 20 seconds the table waited, and then the ready-made lines played with no turn for you: everyone spoke and the conversation ended. Now the table waits 10 seconds more for each villager past two, and when the opening is still late, the ready-made lines take your replies and the AI picks it up from there.
  • Long lines at the table: in a sit-down talk with three or four villagers, a long line ran past the bottom of its box and was hidden under the reply panel, so it looked cut off. Now it carries on in the box, page by page.
  • Villagers stay in the room and in sight: in the mod's scenes (dinners, outings, visits, birthday parties), a villager placed on a tile taken by furniture, an object or a wall was moved to the first free tile in a square around it, which could be on the other side of a wall: they showed up in the next room, off screen or inside the furniture. Now they move to the free tile right next to theirs or to the nearest free floor of your room, never through a wall.
  • Sam's open rehearsal: Sam stood where you couldn't get close, so you couldn't talk to him or start the rehearsal. On a Save the Date day you walk around in, every villager out of reach now moves to the nearest floor you can reach, facing them or beside them, with nobody in the way.
  • The chicken race with Stardew Valley Expanded: you started on the other side of Marnie, where you couldn't talk to Shane and start the race. Now you start next to him.
  • Birthday parties and the citizen of the year dinner that didn't start: walking into the Saloon for Abigail's party or the citizen of the year dinner, or into the lab for Demetrius's party, sometimes played nothing. Now, when the game doesn't start the scene right after you walk in, the mod starts it.
  • Square brackets in the AI's text: when Lewis's speech at the citizen of the year dinner, or a line at a birthday party or an outing, came back from the AI with square brackets (a “[laughs]”, or an empty “[]”), the game couldn't build the scene and it never played. Now square brackets become parentheses.
  • The farm's shelf at Pierre's: with no room near the counter, it went into the family's rooms behind the shop and nobody could find it. Now it goes on the wall nearest the front door, on the shop floor.
